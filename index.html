Biography

Danilo Guesela is a PhD student at the School of Computer and Cyber Sciences at Augusta University and a Principal Software Engineer and Technical Lead at Savannah River Nuclear Solutions (SRNS). His professional and academic interests lie at the intersection of software engineering, artificial intelligence, software safety, information security, and digital transformation of complex industrial systems.

With more than 14 years of experience in software engineering, Danilo has worked across nuclear operations, manufacturing, industrial automation, biotechnology, and academic research. His work spans software architecture, full-stack development, systems integration, process automation, information security, and technical leadership.

At SRNS, Danilo leads a software engineering team responsible for implementing Smart Electronic Procedures (SEP) as part of a broader plant-wide digital transformation initiative. The team works to transition paper-based operational procedures into digital, structured, and optimized workflows. His responsibilities include technical direction, stakeholder collaboration, and the development of software supporting complex production processes, nuclear and chemical material management, and regulatory compliance.

Previously, Danilo held software development and leadership roles at Amcor, Rural Sourcing, Berry Plastics, and Augusta University’s Center for Biotechnology and Genomic Medicine. His experience includes industrial automation, SCADA, PLC-based systems, enterprise applications, manufacturing optimization, and research computing infrastructure.

His doctoral research investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can assist safety engineers with requirements analysis, hazard identification, evidence traceability, and engineering decisions. His SafetyRAG research proposes a Safety Evidence Graph (SEG), provenance-preserving retrieval, claim-level evidence verification, and human-supervised reasoning. The Boeing 737 MAX/MCAS serves as a motivating case study; effectiveness claims remain subject to formal and empirical evaluation.

Danilo holds a Master of Science in Information Security Management from Augusta University (2019) and a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from Indiana University (2012). He aims to advance trustworthy, evidence-grounded software systems for safety-critical environments.

Featured PhD Research

SafetyRAG — Research & Architecture

Evidence-grounded, graph-aware and human-supervised AI assistance for software and system safety engineering.

Active research · Empirical validation pending

Why SafetyRAG?

Safety evidence is distributed across requirements, system designs, hazard analyses, failure analyses, verification artifacts and incident records. SafetyRAG investigates how language models can assist safety engineers while preserving source traceability and human authority over engineering decisions.

Research premise: a generated safety claim is not an accepted engineering finding, and a citation is not proof that its source supports the claim.

Research objectives
  • Represent safety relationships with a typed Safety Evidence Graph (SEG)
  • Retain document identity, versions, evidence spans and lineage
  • Retrieve evidence using semantic, lexical and graph-aware methods
  • Assess claim support and contradictions
  • Require authorized human review before engineering acceptance

Conceptual SafetyRAG architecture

A proposed end-to-end research architecture. The diagram illustrates intended components, not a claim that every component has been implemented or experimentally validated.

Safety Evidence Graph (SEG)

SEG is a proposed typed, attributed, version-aware multigraph connecting engineering entities and provenance-bearing relationship assertions. Distinct assertions can coexist, including contradictory or differently scoped evidence.

GSEG = (V, E, src, dst, τV, τE, α, π, ν)

V: entities; E: uniquely identified edge assertions; src/dst: endpoints; τ: types; α: attributes; π: provenance; ν: version/validity metadata. This is a research definition, not a verified software property.

Provenance and evidence integrity

Evidence should be traceable to the exact artifact version and relevant passage. Source existence, citation resolvability, and semantic support are separate questions. Conflicting or outdated evidence must not be silently treated as current, authoritative support.

Source anchorsVersion awarenessClaim supportContradiction checks

Hybrid retrieval

The proposed retrieval pipeline combines lexical matching, semantic similarity, SEG traversal and safety-aware eligibility constraints. Research questions include whether provenance and graph constraints improve evidence quality compared with strong RAG baselines.

LexicalSemanticGraph-awareEligibility

Human-supervised reasoning

AI-generated findings remain proposals. Qualified engineers review the supporting and contradicting evidence before accepting, revising, rejecting or deferring findings. The proposed protocol separates model generation, evidence assessment and engineering decisions.

Human authorityReview statesAudit trail

Research case study: Boeing 737 MAX / MCAS

The MCAS case motivates research on cross-artifact reasoning: sensor inputs, control logic, flight-control actuation, safety requirements, hazards and incident evidence. It is a research case study, not evidence that SafetyRAG has independently established new accident findings.

AoA sensor→MCAS logic→Stabilizer trim→Aircraft response

Research evaluation — planned, not measured

Planned comparisons include LLM-only, standard RAG, graph-enhanced RAG and full SafetyRAG. Candidate measures include hazard precision/recall, unsupported-claim rate, evidence attribution, traceability, workload and human decision quality.
